## Three structures
1. **Landscape → cell → back (nested magnifiers).** Open on the plate of the island, dive through magnifiers (cheek → cells → DNA → mitochondria) and pull back to the tortoise. *Chosen*: the story literally goes from a 194-year-old animal down to the order inside its mitochondria and back to "maybe he is just good at being old"; the magnifier is the style's own change of scale.
2. A timeline plate (1832 → 1882 → today) with the DNA as a coda. Rejected: the science is the point, the dates are setup.
3. A grain-on-a-thread follow of one methyl mark along the helix. Rejected: too abstract for the hook.

## Logline / arc
A pencil naturalist's plate of the oldest land animal alive, read deeper and deeper until the drawing reaches the tidy marks inside his cells — and then pulls back with a caveat.
Setup (0–28.6: who he is) → descent (28.6–63.5: DNA, telomeres, disordered marks, the exception) → turn (63.5–79.1: ordered marks, but one animal, no proof) → ending (79.1–end: the plate, the question, the name pencilled in the sky).

## Benchmark
Learn: natural-history textbook cutaways and field-guide plates (restraint, one idea per plate, labels on paper tags), the multiplane magnifier of hand-drawn science shorts. Don't take: any plate layout, character, typeface.
