## Three structures
1. **A biography almanac.** One spread per chapter: the egg (1832), the voyage (1882), the record today, the inside (DNA), the caveat. Page turns are the chapter changes, and the book closes at the end. *Chosen.* The story is a biography that turns into a lab report, and a page turn per chapter is the style's own grammar. The almanac's "pull-tab left running" maps onto Jonathan's 194 years: the tortoise card stays up on every spread.
2. **One spread pulled again and again.** The same island spread, with a pull-tab revealing deeper layers (skin → cells → DNA). Rejected: the voyage and the dates need their own spreads.
3. **Two books facing each other** (young tortoise vs. Jonathan). Rejected: that comparison is only one beat (the ordered/disordered marks).

## Logline / arc
A hardcover "life of Jonathan" lies on a desk. It opens on the egg of 1832, sails to Saint Helena, stands the oldest land animal on the page, then opens into his cells, where paper DNA, telomere tabs and tidy mitochondria tell what was found. A last spread admits it is only one animal, and the book closes.
- Setup (0–28.6): who he is.
- Descent (28.6–63.5): the inside.
- Turn (63.5–79.1): ordered marks, then the caveat.
- Ending (79.1–90.8): the question, and the book closing on the name.
