## Three structures
1. **One object, three fates (chosen).** The same wireframe star is scanned three times: as the textbook says (layers explode open, core collapses, supernova light wave), as the data hint (it collapses inward with no flash) and as the question (side by side with the black hole). Between them, the evidence is scanned as its own objects: two black holes on a spacetime grid, then a holographic histogram. Chosen because the style's job is "an object with parts, locked one at a time", and a star *is* an onion of parts; the story's turn is literally "the explode that never happens".
2. A spec sheet of the black-hole population (one object, the histogram, locked part by part). Rejected as the frame: it hides the star, which is the hook.
3. A follow of one gravitational-wave chirp from merger to Earth. Kept as section D only.

## Logline / arc
A scanner opens a massive star like an exploded view and waits for the supernova; then 257 collisions say many stars just fold in on themselves, silently.
Setup (0–27: hook, textbook star, supernova, remnant) → evidence (27–60.8: binary on spacetime grid, 257 rolled, histogram: peak ≈10, gap 12–16) → turn (60.8–72: the star collapses with no flash — "supernova fallida") → caveat (72–83: checklist plates, only "encaja" turns accent) → ending (83–96.9: dots on the grid wink out; star vs. black hole; "LO QUE IGNORAMOS" rolls in; scan-erase).

## Benchmark
Learn: film HUD grammar (target boxes, rolling values, layered depth), CAD exploded views, the one-colour restraint of minimalist sci-fi interfaces. Don't take: any layout, icon, font or colour from them.
